Why This Program Matters

The mechanics of a hackathon are simple. Keeping people showing up, season after season, is the actual problem.

Creative Execution

The season 4 journey, in the creative that carried it

A representative slice of the full-funnel campaign, announcement, registration urgency, phase check-ins, and the finale, all written and designed in-house with a graphic designer and technical writer.

Announce

"Stay tuned"

Sets the tone before registration even opens, builds anticipation off the back of the previous season's success.

Register

"Tick-tock! Time is running out"

Prize amounts made visible upfront alongside the countdown, giving people a concrete reason to register before the deadline.

Build

"It's Design Time!"

Each build phase got its own check-in banner, keeping teams oriented across the 6-week build window, not just at kickoff and demo day.

Finale

"Who is the Winner?"

Builds toward demo day itself, the moment teams' 6 weeks of work gets judged live by leadership panelists.

Recognize

Prize winners announced

Closing the loop publicly, recognition mattered as much as the prize money for keeping the culture of participation alive.

The Approach

Operationalizing a vision into a repeatable program

Structure

Turned a leadership concept into a repeatable format

Gave the program a consistent phased journey, Ideate, Collaborate, Design, Prototype, Test, Iterate, Demo, so every season ran on the same reliable spine, regardless of that year's specific challenges.

Campaign

Ran the full creative campaign, every season

Owned pre-event hype, registration urgency, in-flight phase check-ins, and recap content each season, working with a graphic designer and technical writer to keep the identity consistent and fresh at once.

Experience

Made participation feel valued, not just judged

Arranged swag, certificates, and prize logistics, and helped run the felicitation moments that turned a scoring exercise into a genuine culture of innovation.

Convert

Connected demos to a real path to patents

Coordinated with the Legal/Patent team, who reviewed demo recordings and transcripts each season to identify patent-worthy ideas, with 20+ patents filed collectively across all seasons, awarded jointly to the participating teams.

Ripple effect

Other business units within the company saw SYSrupt's success and started running their own hackathons modeled on it, the format outgrew its original scope and became a reference point across the organization.
