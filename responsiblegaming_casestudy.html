Design & Experience, UI/UX, Responsible Gaming

An integrated Responsible Gaming tool
designed for the Illinois market.

A built-in limit-setting tool that lets slot players set time and spend limits before play, reducing impulsive, unbounded sessions without getting in the way of the game.

RoleUX/UI Designer
MarketIllinois, US
TeamCreative director, PM, UX/UI, Engineering
2-minute read
Problem
Players had no easy, built-in way to set time or spend limits before playing.
Approach
Researched problem-gambling behavior, built personas, and benchmarked against a competitor tool.
Execution
Designed the flow, UI, and a shared design system so engineering could implement it directly.
Result
Improved on the competitor across 5 UX dimensions, with limits now visible and set upfront.

User Research

Three personas grounded the design in real behavior

Built from secondary research into problem-gambling motivations, used to keep the design focused on real risk patterns rather than assumptions.

Persona 01

The Casual Player

Plays occasionally for entertainment; benefits most from visible, low-friction limit reminders rather than hard blocks.

Persona 02

The Frequent Player

Plays regularly and is more prone to losing track of time and spend; needs limits that are easy to set and hard to ignore.

Persona 03

The At-Risk Player

Shows early signs of impulsive, loss-chasing behavior; the primary user the limit-setting tool is designed to protect.

Before / After

Benchmarked against a competitor, dimension by dimension

Five UX dimensions compared directly against a competitor's live product to find where our design could remove friction.

1. Landing page clarity
Confusion before login vs. a clear opt-in path
Competitor
Our Design

Competitor surfaces account info and real-time limits before login, which risks confusing new users. Ours lets players continue without login, or opt in to track and set limits.

2. Hide & show
No privacy control vs. a hide option for real-time data
Competitor
Our Design

The competitor has no way to conceal real-time spend and time data on screen. Ours adds a hide option for players who don't want that visible.

3. Use of labels
Unlabeled data vs. clearly separated, labeled sections
Competitor
Our Design

Competitor's real-time limits aren't labeled, costing users time to interpret. Ours separates and labels limits into two clear sections.

4. Time on task
More clicks to set a limit vs. fewer clicks, upfront
Competitor
Our Design

Setting a limit on the competitor's product takes more clicks. Ours surfaces the set-limits option upfront, reducing steps to complete.

5. Emphasis on call-to-action
Buttons easy to miss vs. buttons designed to be found
Competitor
Our Design

The competitor gives no visual emphasis to its call-to-action buttons. Ours emphasizes "change limits" and "set limits" so they're found quickly.

The Approach

Research-first, benchmarked, built for handoff

Research

Grounded the design in problem-gambling behavior

Used secondary research to understand at-risk play patterns, then built three personas to keep design decisions tied to real user needs rather than assumptions.

Map

Modeled the user flow and journey before designing screens

Mapped how a player would realistically reach the goal of setting a limit, and where that moment sits inside the broader play journey.

Benchmark

Compared directly against a live competitor product

Evaluated a competitor's limit-setting experience across five concrete dimensions, landing page clarity, privacy controls, labeling, time on task, and CTA emphasis, to find real, specific gaps rather than generic ones.

Systemize

Handed off through a shared design system

Built a design system alongside the interface itself, so engineering could implement, and later extend, the limit-setting tool without re-deriving decisions per screen.

Illustrative representations and screens generalized for public presentation due to NDA.
